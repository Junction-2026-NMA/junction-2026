SISU
The Agentic Financial Operating System | Junction 2026 | Complete Project
Specification

Working name: Sisu. Product and implementation brief intended for handoff to Claude Code.
This describes the proposed final product, target user, technical architecture, demonstration,
datasets, sponsor integrations, constraints and submission requirements. It is a product
specification, not a claim that the product has already been built.


1. Executive concept and pitch
Sisu is a mobile-first, privacy-conscious financial agent for young Finnish and European
adults who struggle to translate their banking history into confident financial decisions.
Existing banking interfaces explain what happened. Sisu identifies financially meaningful
patterns, investigates current real-world alternatives, simulates what changes would mean
for the customer's future, teaches the relevant concepts and completes the tedious parts of
the next step—subject to user approval.
Core proposition: “Your bank knows your past. Sisu helps change your future.” Supporting
hook: “We find the money you didn't know you were losing, and help you do something
about it.” The concept is an agentic financial coach, not a stock-picking bot, passive
dashboard or generic chatbot.
Primary problem: Young adults have valuable transaction histories, fragmented financial
knowledge and numerous online options, but limited ability or time to join them into
evidence-based, personalized action. A bank has deep context about the customer's money;
the web has today's prices, rules, options and opportunities. Sisu bridges those two worlds,
with visible provenance and guardrails.


Who benefits
Primary users: Finnish young adults around 18–30, including people moving out, renting,
budgeting for the first time, considering a mortgage, starting an emergency fund or learning
about investing. Secondary users: families and other retail-bank customers facing
recurring-bill, rent, savings and mortgage decisions. Institutional stakeholder: S-Pankki and
its S-mobiili digital banking ecosystem. Technology stakeholders: Exa, NVIDIA, Verda,
ElevenLabs, Context.dev, and optionally Modal. The developer team and judges are demo
stakeholders.
Why S-Pankki: S-Bank describes itself as Finland's fourth-largest bank with 3.5 million
customers and reports that S-mobiili is used weekly by one in three Finnish adults. Its
challenge explicitly prioritizes trusted conversational banking, financial literacy,
personalization, three spending baskets, what-if guidance, mobile UX and a realistic path to
integration. Sisu should feel like a native extension to S-mobiili, not an unrelated startup
product.
2. Product: what Sisu actually does

A. Financial X-Ray — deeply personalized banking intelligence
Users choose a synthetic S-Pankki customer. Sisu analyzes transactions, income and
outflows, merchant recurrence, balances over time, cash-flow seasonality, potentially
avoidable expenses and unusual changes. It assigns expenses to the three required
pedagogical baskets: (1) mandatory regular costs such as rent, (2) mandatory irregular costs
such as repairs, and (3) everyday discretionary spending that quietly accumulates. The UI
allows open-ended questions over the user's own data, such as “Where did my money go?”,
“How much did I spend on taxis last month?”, “What kind of spender am I?”, “What can I
safely spend this week?” and “What if I spent 20% less on dining?”
This layer must compute figures from structured queries and deterministic financial code. An
LLM can interpret the question and explain results, but must not invent balances, amounts,
transaction dates, merchant names, eligibility or investment returns. Show traceable
transaction groups behind every material claim. Any inferred recurring bill or insight should
carry confidence and allow correction.


B. Opportunity Hunter — Exa-powered internet butler
The agent turns patterns into possible actions: compare telecom or energy contracts;
investigate rent alternatives and applicable rules; examine public support/benefit
opportunities; compare bank accounts, savings products or mortgage terms; research an
apartment; inspect ETF factsheets and fee structures. Exa must retrieve live facts that model
training alone cannot know, with URLs, access dates, quoted facts or extracted fields,
caveats and source quality. The result is not “Here are three links.” It is a completed
research task: screened alternatives, comparative calculations, relevant eligibility or
switching requirements, and a prepared next step.
Agent workflow: recognize opportunity → establish current baseline from bank data →
formulate targeted web queries with minimum necessary information → search Exa →
optionally parse candidate pages/PDFs using Context.dev → cross-check current offers and
terms → calculate net benefit and confidence → explain trade-offs → draft a message,
negotiation request, application checklist or simulated instruction → request explicit user
approval → execute only in a safe connected or mocked environment → record outcome
and source provenance.


C. Financial Futures — personalized what-if simulations
A distinctive interactive “Financial Multiverse” compares baseline and alternative futures.
Start with moving out: “Can I afford an apartment in Helsinki while still saving and investing?”
Sisu combines cash-flow history, live rental research, reasonable assumptions about
housing costs, an emergency buffer and the impact on investable surplus. Other scenarios:
a 15% rent increase, job loss for three months, cutting discretionary spending, renegotiating
a bill or mortgage, making a monthly savings contribution, or choosing between debt
repayment and investing.
Visual presentation: side-by-side branching futures, live slider adjustments, projected cash
flow, emergency runway, safe-to-spend and safe-to-save estimates, total costs, opportunity
cost and future wealth. Treat return assumptions as scenarios, not predictions. Show
uncertainty, potential investment loss, fees, taxes and inflation where relevant. User changes
one input and sees both the calculation and the coach's explanation update immediately.


D. Investment and wealth education
Extend the coach beyond budgeting into emergency funds, the cost of debt, cash versus
investments, diversification, index funds and ETF composition, fees, inflation, investment
time horizons and risk tolerance. The user can ask what happens if €125 per month
previously spent on dining is saved or invested. Use sourced factsheets through Exa and
verified formula-based projections, not personalized speculative stock picks. Investment
content is educational, with clear assumptions and no autonomous real-money trading in the
hackathon MVP.


E. Embedded financial literacy
Financial literacy must be part of each decision, not a detached course tab. Sisu teaches at
the moment of relevance. It explains why expenses fall in each of the three S-Pankki
baskets; asks “Predict before you reveal” microquestions before displaying a simulation;
offers quick explanations of compounding, liquidity, emergency funds, opportunity cost,
taxes, risk and mortgage affordability; and remembers which concepts the user has already
understood. Judge-visible learning: show an initial misconception, user prediction,
evidence-based outcome and short tailored explanation. Measure progress through scenario
decisions and short comprehension checks, not arbitrary engagement metrics.


F. Persistent personalization and proactive work
With consent, remember customer goals, past conversations, preferences, corrections and
prior approvals, such as wanting an apartment near Helsinki metro, a 30-minute commute
ceiling or a planned emergency fund. Memory is stored in a controlled backend database
and retrieved when relevant, not automatically baked into model weights. In a more mature
deployment the agent can monitor offers, policy changes or recurring financial issues and
notify the user. For Junction, demonstrate a persisted preference across sessions or an
on-demand fresh research task rather than claiming continuously autonomous monitoring
that does not exist.


3. Flagship 90-second live booth experience
1 | Select an attractive synthetic S-Pankki customer profile on a mobile-first interface. Show
animated transaction analysis, spending baskets and 2–3 verifiable opportunities. A reviewer
can inspect the specific records and see exactly why the agent flagged an expense.
2 | Reviewer asks by voice: “Can you find a cheaper option for this recurring bill?”
Alternatively: “Can I afford to move to Helsinki?” or “What would happen if I lost my job for
three months?” ElevenLabs transcribes and speaks; Nemotron plans tool calls; Python/SQL
computes baseline.
3 | Live Exa search retrieves up-to-date alternatives or relevant support rules, and
Context.dev extracts structured offer conditions, fees or PDF terms when needed. Project
source links and “retrieved just now” evidence beside the actual comparisons. Judges
should be able to vary the query; never rely on screenshots of canned results.
4 | The Financial Multiverse dramatically branches into “do nothing”, “switch/save”, and
“save/invest” alternatives, with precise math and clear assumptions. Voice questions or
sliders change figures in real time. Insert a teaching interaction: “Which future is safer if you
suddenly lose income?”
5 | The agent prepares a negotiation email, application checklist or safe simulated
recurring-savings instruction. Reviewer approves; the simulated action is completed and the
financial future recalculates. The exact on-screen payoff: personal-data insight → live factual
research → understandable recommendation → prepared/approved action → measurable
simulated impact.
Booth design requirements: a striking, polished mobile UI; readable large-screen mirror;
speed; judge-friendly example profiles; one-click resets; strong loading and failure states;
evidence drawer; voice optional with text fallback; robust handling of arbitrary judge queries.
Avoid faked live calls. Demonstrate an actual endpoint request and the output it produced.


4. Layered technical architecture
The architecture is deliberately modular. The secure customer data and financial calculations
are independent of the conversational language model. External web tools see only the
smallest query needed; they do not receive raw transaction histories. All tool results have
source and freshness metadata. Use a user-approved action boundary before any external
write or simulated banking change.
Architecture diagram — the complete stack




Layer 1 — Experience: Next.js/React mobile-first application that can plausibly be embedded
in S-mobiili; spending charts, interactive future projections, sources, education prompts,
opportunity feed and permission/approval cards. Responsive web deployment and QR-link
access for peer reviewers.
Layer 2 — Conversation: ElevenLabs voice input/output with low-latency turn-taking; text
remains the primary accessible fallback. ElevenLabs connects to the application
orchestration endpoint, not directly to raw bank tables. Model voice/agent features should be
tested for latency and reliability.
Layer 3 — Reasoning and orchestration: NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super VL as the required
core reasoning model for the NVIDIA track. It interprets intents, chooses tools, handles
grounded research, reads supporting PDFs/screenshots where useful, structures answers
and initiates controlled task plans. Keep an inspectable trace of its tool calls and why each
was made. NVIDIA NeMo is a training/customization framework, not itself the inference
model; optional offline evaluation or tuning is a later extension, not a hard dependency.
Layer 4A — Banking intelligence: S-Pankki synthetic transactions and customer data in
DuckDB/PostgreSQL/Supabase, queried via constrained APIs and deterministic SQL/Python
calculations. Mortgage event data lives in a separate joined data model. Implement secure
authorization and per-customer filtering before the model receives any results.
Layer 4B — Live retrieval: Exa Search API (fast/balanced/deep) finds recent offers, laws,
relevant public resources, factsheets and comparable listings; Contents retrieves readable
pages and source excerpts. Websets and Monitors may support future verified discovery
and recurring checks. Preserve links, retrieval time and evidence.
Layer 4C — Structured web: Context.dev is NOT conversational memory. It extracts selected
live pages or documents into typed JSON or Markdown, e.g. provider, monthly price,
introductory-period duration, setup fees, cancellation conditions, eligibility criteria and
effective date. An endpoint call must be core to the live demo for its prize, and visibly drive
the actual comparison. Check success flags, freshness (maxAgeMs: 0 when necessary) and
extraction validation.
Layer 4D — Memory: Supabase/Postgres + vector retrieval where useful; session state,
consented personal preferences, goals, explanations and decisions. Separate short-term
context from durable memory, encrypt and allow deletion/correction. The model does not
need to retrain itself after each interaction.
Layer 5 — Financial simulation: audited Python/TypeScript computations for cash-flow
trends, safe-to-spend, risk buffers, expected expenses, savings trajectories, debt trade-offs,
investment projections and scenario sensitivities. Do not let Nemotron perform critical
arithmetic without tool verification. Store assumptions and provenance with each result.
Layer 6 — Action workflow: State-machine-driven agent for research → recommendation →
draft → approval → safe execution. Working demo examples: draft a landlord/bill negotiation
email, create a benefit application checklist, generate a provider-switch request, or create a
savings rule inside a mocked S-mobiili environment. Confirm before sending, changing a
record or initiating any financial instruction. Every action has a visible status and audit log.
Layer 7 — Compute / hosting: Verda European cloud to host Nemotron via its
NVIDIA-associated endpoint or on a suitable GPU deployment, plus agent backend on CPU
instances and other services. Verda is not mandatory for product functionality, but is a
strategic match for keeping inference under European operational control. If custom model
weights/fine-tuning are required, use appropriately provisioned GPU instances/containers
rather than assuming a managed endpoint supports arbitrary fine-tunes. A secure bank
deployment additionally needs due diligence, regulated outsourcing controls, encryption,
isolation and operational resilience.
Alternative infrastructure: Modal could serve the model, tool execution or isolated agent
code, but competing simultaneously on Verda and Modal can dilute the clear European
infrastructure narrative. Recommend Verda as primary; use Modal only for a distinct
compute-intensive component that materially improves the demo. Avoid a fragmented
architecture deployed across many clouds solely to enter prizes.
5. Dataset specification and handling
Official synthetic dataset repository: https://github.com/hackjunction/Junction-2026-S-pankki
. The provided S-Pankki PDF brief is five pages. Page 2 summarizes the mobile-first coach,
user questions, three spending baskets and MyData principles; page 3 specifies ten
transaction fields; page 4 specifies customer fields; page 5 specifies mortgage event fields.


Transaction data — tx_junction_transactions.csv
The transaction file is about 346 MB, Git LFS managed, with columns: customer_id;
bank_account_id; tx_date; tx_timestamp; category; tx_amount_eur (negative for outflows
and positive for inflows); balance_after_eur; transaction_type; merchant_id; mcc_code.
Missing merchant/MCC can be encoded as '.' or blank for non-card transfers. Preserve dates
and signs correctly. Verify download size: a roughly 134-byte pointer that begins “version
https://git-lfs.github.com/spec/v1” is NOT the CSV. Use Git LFS or the repository's Download
Raw option.


Customer data — tx_customers_junction.csv
Contains customer_id, customer_postal_code and customer_age. Postal code MUST be
treated as text to retain leading zeroes (e.g. 00990, 00530). The combination supports
segment views and regional benchmarks, but age or postcode alone must not be treated as
proof of benefit eligibility, personal circumstances or risk tolerance.


Mortgage datasets
mtg_negotiation_history.csv includes event-level mortgage application data with 16 named
fields: mortgage_customer_id; timestamp; event_type; property_id; property_type;
property_price_estimate; loan_amount; equity_amount; collateral_type; collateral_value;
collateral_status; decision; rejection_reason; expense_type; expense_amount; note. The
history captures events such as expense_recorded, collateral_added and
collateral_valuation, as well as possible final approvals/rejections and reasons.
mtg_tx_customer_link_and_final_mtg_infio.csv joins mortgage_customer_id to transaction
customer_id and provides final application outcome information. Treat historical application
status, requested loan and collateral value separately from current outstanding mortgage
balance, interest rate and monthly payment; the brief does not establish that those
present-day parameters exist for every user. Ask for missing values or label demo
assumptions clearly. This mortgage view supports a possible flagship Negotiation/Readiness
Agent and deeper bank-specific personalization.
Initial pipelines: load synthetic CSVs with UTF-8 BOM-aware encoding (utf-8-sig); normalize
customer/account keys and timestamps; index by customer/date; precompute recurrence
and rolling cash-flow features; redact or minimize fields before inference; expose read-only
financial-query functions. Do not assume all external offers or investments are linked in the
bank dataset.
6. Three concrete agent workflows

Workflow A — Recurring Expense Negotiator (primary
end-to-end execution demo)
The bank data shows a recurring telecom expense. Sisu identifies the charge and frequency,
asks whether the customer wants alternatives, uses Exa to find current suitable Finnish
plans, uses Context.dev structured extraction to retrieve monthly price, contract term,
restrictions, setup/switching fees and eligibility, computes net annual savings, cites each
source, drafts a provider inquiry or cancellation/switching checklist, requests approval, and
executes a safe simulated action. Then the projection updates. Avoid claiming merchant
identity where dataset only has a synthetic merchant ID; map to a clear fictional illustrative
provider or collect the actual provider from the user.


Workflow B — First Apartment / Benefits Butler
The user wants to move out in Helsinki. Sisu uses spending and cash-flow data, Exa's live
listings and relevant public guidance (including Kela or other official sources), optionally
Context.dev extraction, and a what-if engine to show feasible rents and trade-offs. It finds
qualifying-looking support possibilities but does not state final eligibility without necessary
verified facts. It can create a research-backed apartment shortlist, inquiry drafts and an
application checklist; user approves all outbound actions.


Workflow C — Mortgage Readiness / Negotiation
For customers with mortgage application events, join historical mortgage steps with
cash-flow and income signals; identify application status and known constraints; compare
current mortgage information or offers sourced through Exa; display terms, fees, affordability
and future payments; prepare lender questions or a negotiation brief. Do not imply that
historical mortgage application data reveals current loan APR or that another bank will
approve a customer. Mortgage product suitability and eligibility remain subject to lender
assessment.


7. Trust, privacy, European AI and model customization
A key product argument is that S-Pankki's underused financial data is the defensible
intelligence. Nemotron is an open-weight model family that can run on controlled
infrastructure, and Verda operates European cloud services. That enables a potentially more
controlled data-processing and deployment architecture than relying exclusively on
third-party hosted proprietary inference. HOWEVER, third-party APIs do not inherently
transfer ownership of data or necessarily violate bank confidentiality; privacy depends on
contracts, processing terms and technical safeguards. Treat sovereign/private inference as a
design objective, not an unverified compliance guarantee.
Data minimization: raw transactions stay in controlled storage; the model is given
customer-authorized aggregates or selected facts, not unlimited unrestricted table access.
Exa/Context.dev queries should not contain personal transaction histories, identifiers or
private financial circumstances except carefully approved minimal context. Use encrypted
storage and TLS, least-privilege tool scopes, logs, per-customer authorization, explicit
consent and data retention rules. Separate the synthetic hackathon sandbox from any
prospective production environment.
NeMo training is optional. Continuous on-the-fly fine-tuning on customers' private
conversations is NOT the intended hackathon architecture. Personalization should primarily
come from secure retrieval and consented persistent memory. Future bank-specific
fine-tuning could train on permissioned, appropriately processed proprietary data using
evaluation pipelines, subject to security, licensing and model risk management. NVIDIA
judges require actual Nemotron 3.5 Super VL use; merely mentioning NeMo is insufficient.
Safe execution: financial advice is educational, calculations are auditable, sources are
linked, uncertainty is displayed, and potentially consequential steps require customer
approval. No unauthorized emails, forms, bank transfers, account modifications or trades.
Mock execution must be clearly labeled. A complete action is a verified state change or
prepared/sendable artifact—not a mere conversational promise.

10. Scope and product differentiation
The central innovative combination is: underused transaction intelligence + open-weight
bank-controlled reasoning + live web investigation + structured extraction + financial
simulations + contextual teaching + concrete agent action. No single component is asserted
as globally unprecedented. The memorable innovation lies in integrating them into a verified,
personalized end-to-end workflow that works with real prompts.
Primary working experience: recurring expense discovery and agentic switching/negotiation
in a safe mock environment. Secondary working experience: “Can I afford to move out?” with
live rental research and financial futures. Tertiary optional experience: mortgage readiness
and investment education. It is better to have a compelling complete workflow and two
robust adjacent queries than many incomplete features.
Explicit non-goals for the hackathon: autonomous live stock trading; guaranteed financial
returns; unsolicited sending of private data to web APIs; continuous uncontrolled fine-tuning
on private customer records; unverified legal/benefit eligibility judgments; direct live S-Pankki
integrations without credentials/authorization; claims that simulated actions are real bank
transfers or successful provider switches.

Parallel development and Git rules
1. Create main protected branch, and feature/frontend, feature/financial-engine,
feature/agent-orchestration branches. Commit initial app skeleton and shared contracts to
main first.
2. Use pull requests (PRs) instead of directly pushing work to main. Each PR needs a short
summary, successful build/tests, example request/response for changed endpoints, and a
reviewer from another role. Small PRs throughout the hackathon beat one giant final merge.
3. Both backend owners work under separate directories/modules to minimize collisions. UI
owner depends only on shared contracts, not another teammate's temporary
implementation. Use mock adapters until integrations are live.
4. One person acts as integration lead (recommend the backend/data owner, because
calculations and API correctness are core). Integration lead merges after checks,
coordinates environment variables and ensures frontend/backend contract stability; do not
make this person rewrite teammates' features.
5. Continuous integration: run install, lint, typecheck, unit tests and a smoke test for the
public demo. Require a reproducible .env.example without secret values. Never commit
keys. Use staging deploy previews and a known-good main branch.
6. Integration milestones: first, transaction data + UI stub + agent stub all obey the shared
contract; next, complete one real end-to-end expense opportunity with live Exa/Context and
calculation; then, connect ElevenLabs and Verda; finally, test peer-reviewer prompts,
rehearse and freeze optional additions. Plan parallel work, not sequential handoffs.
7. Conflict prevention: Git worktrees are optional for multiple agents working from one
machine, but each teammate's separate clone and branch is sufficient. Rebase or merge
main regularly, communicate schema changes immediately and never silently rename
endpoint fields. Keep a rollback tag for the last working demo.
8. Scope discipline: if short on time, prioritize the complete live Expense Negotiator demo,
then the move-out scenario, and only then add additional investment/mortgage or sponsor
embellishments. Assign one designated speaker and a second person as live-demo operator
while the third handles judge questions and failure recovery.


Cross-functional demo definition of done
A peer reviewer can open the deployed URL, choose a real synthetic customer, see correctly
computed spending insights, ask a new question by text or voice, watch Nemotron invoke
tools, inspect Exa and Context.dev sources, change a scenario, understand one financial
lesson, approve a prepared simulated action and see the financial impact update. Everyone
can explain their component and the shared flow in 30 seconds.
